Choose your topics

Which topics do residents show up for?

  1. Trees, road works and playgrounds draw few residents.

    Each tile is a topic, sized by its share of all participants. Cities run plenty of these projects, and few residents join them: playgrounds are 4% of projects and 2% of participants.

  2. Their safety, their squares and their city’s budget draw more.

    Residents turn up for a decision they can see: about their place, their money or their safety.

  3. A budget residents can spend draws the most.

    Participatory budgets are 3.2% of projects and 7.1% of participants. Public votes for local awards are 1% of projects and almost 4% of participants. A light vote gets people onto your platform, and they come back for bigger decisions.

Three topics are rising fast, each for a reason you can plan for.

  1. Community safety

    It doubled in a year: cities must renew their safety rules, and they ask residents first. In Harderwijk, in the Netherlands, 823 residents marked on a map where they feel unsafe.

  2. Public transport

    Responsibility for public transport is moving to the local level. In Wokingham, in the United Kingdom, 1,814 people helped shape the future bus network.

  3. Citizens’ panels

    Cities are replacing advisory committees with permanent panels of residents. New Westminster, in Canada, made its community assembly permanent. See how Newham runs community assemblies

For your 2027 plan

Put decisions residents can see on the calendar.

  1. Turn decisions into questions with options.

    A vote on clear options draws more people than comments on a finished plan. Give residents something to choose between. See how a participatory budget does it.

  2. Learn from cities facing the same challenge.

    Another city has already run the project you are about to plan. Start from what worked there. Browse the case studies.

  3. Plan for what is coming.

    Check which rules your city renews in 2027 and which responsibilities it takes on. Residents will want a say there.
