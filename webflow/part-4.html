Step 03

Maximize reach

How do you reach more residents through the year?

  1. Engage all year, and you reach far more residents.

    This is our strongest finding. We compared each city with itself, year by year. In a sporadic year, a city launches projects in one or two quarters, then goes quiet.

  2. The same city, always on, reaches 5.3 times as many residents.

    Always on means launching projects in three or four quarters. The gain showed up in 85% of the 220 cities we could compare. Each project still drew almost as many people, so more projects meant more residents, and more of them came back.

  3. Launch your biggest projects in September or January.

    A September launch draws 1.8 times as many people as a June launch. January draws 1.6 times. June runs straight into the summer. In September and January, people are back at work and school, with a full season ahead.

For your 2027 plan

Keep something open all year.

  1. Count your empty quarters.

    Look at this year’s calendar and mark the quarters when residents had nothing to take part in. Fill those first in 2027.

  2. Move your biggest launch.

    If a big consultation is set for June, check whether it can wait until September. If it cannot, bring it forward to January.

  3. Pick one always-on format.

    A proposals page, a panel or a standing survey. One is enough to start. See how citizen proposals work.

Go deeper: what the widest-reaching cities run

In the top 25% of cities by share of adults reached, 17% of projects are recurring formats such as participatory budgets, proposals and panels. In the other cities it is 10%. Residents also come back more often in always-on cities: 6.8% of participants took part in a second project, against 2.8% in sporadic ones.

The month pattern is a correlation. It changes by year, and within a single city the months sit closer together, so part of the effect is which cities launch when.
